# Friction, not walls, wins over casual scrollers

The best free, open-source anti-doomscrolling app for casual Android users would lead with two things: **a short pause with a prominent "close the app" button that appears whenever a distracting app opens**, and **surgical blocking of Reels, Shorts and similar feeds that leaves DMs, search and the rest of the app working**. Self-chosen, costly-to-override limits, scheduled notification digests, optional grayscale and an optional minimalist launcher come next. Delay-plus-dismiss friction has the strongest evidence of any consumer intervention: **57% fewer target-app openings** over six weeks in a 280-person field experiment ([Grüning et al. 2023, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2213114120)) and **about 36% less daily social-media time** in an independent government RCT ([Danish Competition and Consumer Authority 2025](https://en.kfst.dk/media/5t4dscjt/20250619-disrupting-social-media-habits.pdf)). Feed-level blocking is the feature Android users praise most and the one iOS cannot offer ([one sec help](https://feedback.one-sec.app/help/articles/0145042-remove-in-app-distractions-reels-shorts-android)). Competitors charge for exactly these features: one sec's free tier covers one app ([one sec](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/whats-included-in-one-sec-pro)), AppBlock's free tier caps quick blocks at three apps ([AppBlock](https://appblock.app/premium/)), and Opal is reported at $99.99/yr. ScreenZen shows a fully free, tip-funded app can reach 1M+ installs at 4.8★ ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.screenzen&hl=en_US)). The main risks are operational rather than conceptual. Accessibility-based detection breaks when Instagram or YouTube update, OEM battery killers stop services silently, Google Play's AccessibilityService review is demanding, and Android 17's Advanced Protection Mode revokes accessibility from apps like this one. The right design is therefore a UsageStats-based core that works without Accessibility, with Accessibility as an opt-in upgrade. Google's own **Pause Point** (Android 17) now gives a 10-second pause for free, but only on recent Pixels and a OnePlus beta, so the open-source app has to do more than pause: block feeds inside apps, escalate friction, give each session a time budget, and run on every Android 8+ phone. Most evidence comes from short studies, often run with one sec's developers, and effects end when the tool is switched off. That makes **long-term, low-annoyance retention the real product goal**.

## Ten findings that should shape the product

|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening friction with an easy exit is the best-evidenced intervention.** In the one sec experiments, the dismiss option and the delay did the work, and the reflective message "was not effective" ([PNAS 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2213114120)). Request no INTERNET permission and no account. Put no donation link in the Play build. |

## Paywalls guard exactly what casual users need

There are four groups of competitors: OS built-ins (free, but easy to override and device-gated); friction apps (one sec, ScreenZen, Clearspace); blockers and trackers (AppBlock, StayFree, Opal, Lock Me Out); and launchers (Niagara, Minimalist Phone, Olauncher, Focus Launcher). Since 2024 a wave of single-purpose Reels/Shorts blockers has also appeared. Almost every serious Android product depends on AccessibilityService. The few open-source options are either power-user oriented (Curbox), of uncertain maintenance status (Mindful), or brand-new and F-Droid-only (Focus Launcher).

Three patterns stand out. First, the paywall almost always sits on scope and strength (how many apps, strict mode, advanced in-app filters) and not on the basic idea, so a free app wins by being generous where others ration. Second, generosity tracks ratings: ScreenZen (free), AppBlock (usable free tier) and Lock Me Out (generous free scheduling) sit at 4.6–4.8★, while ad-heavy or subscription-pushing apps cluster lower. That correlation is drawn from listings, not a controlled comparison. Third, the open-source field is thin where it matters. Mindful's maintenance status is ambiguous, Curbox has never shipped on Play, and Focus Launcher is weeks old. No FOSS app combines Play distribution, casual-grade polish, friction and feed-level blocking.

## Delay-plus-exit has the strongest evidence; dashboards the weakest

The evidence tags in the feature list below follow a simple scale. **Strong** means multiple randomized or field experiments, including at least one run independently of a vendor. **Moderate** means one RCT, or several small or uncontrolled studies pointing the same way. **Weak** means null results, very small samples, or perception-only data. **Unknown** means no behavioral study was found. All of it should be read with care. A 62-paper meta-analysis found digital self-control tools have "a small to medium effect", but evaluations are "nearly always based on short-term experiments" lacking control groups ([Monge Roffarello & De Russis 2023](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2972709)). The Cambridge review could demonstrate only "short-term shifts in behaviour" and called for independent trials with 6-month follow-up ([Yap et al.](https://theprudencetrust.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Smartphone-and-Social-Media-Interventions.pdf)).

**Friction is the clear leader, with a caveat about independence.** The PNAS experiment, the CHI 2024 log study of 1,039 one sec users and the Danish adolescent RCT all point the same way. In the log study, users continued to the app 67% of the time and dismissed it 33% ([Haliburton et al.](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)). In the Danish trial, the Waiting arm cut sessions by about 60% but made the remaining sessions 24–40% longer ([DCCA](https://en.kfst.dk/media/5t4dscjt/20250619-disrupting-social-media-habits.pdf)). Friction therefore cuts how often people open an app more than how long they stay, which is why it should be paired with a session budget. All three main studies involve one sec's developers or app, though the Danish one was run by a government agency. The Cambridge review's table also cites a one sec RCT with n=719, which conflicts with the PNAS paper's N=280 and is unresolved.

**Effortful friction works but costs goodwill.** Typing a self-affirmation before opening cut use by more than 50% (N=54) ([TypeOut](https://pi.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/lab/people/YuntaoWang/publications/chi-2022-type-out/)). Input-level friction beat a lockout and was accepted more often (61.6% vs 36.3%), but it carried a higher workload ([InteractOut](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16668v2)). Stricter lockouts cut more time (−74 vs −32 min/day), but frustration and perceived coercion rose with strictness, and a weak, escalating lockout was the most preferred ([GoalKeeper](https://ic.kaist.ac.kr/files/papers/kim2019goalkeeper.pdf)). A perception-only workshop found guilt-based "emotional" frictions were seen as manipulative ([Almoallim & Sas 2025](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/231466/1/Are_All_Design_Frictions_Equal_BHCI_2025.pdf)).

**Removing feeds and blocking apps works, but users substitute.** Blocking Instagram or YouTube for one to two weeks cut phone time by 27–44 min/day, but other social apps rose 15–23%. Only the two-week Instagram block persisted afterward ([Aridor 2022](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/sem2023/eco_platforms/aridor.pdf)). Removing Facebook's newsfeed helped students stay on task but raised fear of missing out (N=58) ([Lyngs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04180v2)). Full abstinence shows near-zero average wellbeing effects (g≈0.03) ([Lemahieu et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40038410/)). **No RCT has tested "remove only Reels/Shorts."** The feature's evidence rating is extrapolated from these studies and from user demand.

## Master feature list across 13 categories

Each table gives Priority (MVP / v1.x / later), Evidence, Appeal to casual users, Difficulty, Permissions and Play-policy risk, and which competitors offer the feature, with **(P)** marking paid-only and **(ads)** marking ad-supported. Permission abbreviations: **UA** = Usage Access (`PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS`, low policy risk); **SAW** = draw over other apps; **A11y** = AccessibilityService (high risk: Play declaration, prominent disclosure and video, revoked by Android 17 AAPM); **NLS** = notification listener; **WSS** = `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` granted via Shizuku or ADB; **FGS** = `specialUse` foreground service (declared in Play Console). Competitor attributions come from the cited listings in the comparison table and the cross-app inventory. Where one cell says "?", a listing did not confirm the feature.

### Friction and intentional use

This is the core of the product. Pause Point raises the baseline expectation, so the app has to match its 10-second pause, breathing, intention and alternatives ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/phones/android/the-new-pause-point-feature-in-android-17-wants-to-stop-you-doomscrolling-and-its-something-ill-be-turning-on-right-away)) and go further with escalation and budgets. One sec's study default was a 6 s breathing delay, adjustable from 3 to 60 s, which about 90% of users kept ([Haliburton et al.](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haliburton2024chi/haliburton2024chi.pdf)).

### In-app content blocking

This is the strongest pull for casual users ("I want Instagram DMs, not Reels") and the hardest to keep working. Detection matches resource IDs or layout fingerprints such as Instagram's `reel_recycler`, then backs out or overlays the screen ([Nudge](https://dev.to/diven_rastdus_c5af27d68f3/block-youtube-shorts-and-reels-on-android-keep-the-app-1b9h)). Shorts-Blocker warns that "App updates may break detection" ([GitHub](https://github.com/atick-faisal/Shorts-Blocker)). Because of substitution effects, a shared short-video allowance across all apps fits the evidence better than blocking one app at a time ([Aridor](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/sem2023/eco_platforms/aridor.pdf)).

### Visual de-stimulation, including the optional launcher

Grayscale has no public API. ## Fifteen features that would make it the category's best

The ranking weighs evidence, appeal to casual users and the size of the market gap (free where others charge, or done better). The first five carry the product. The rest turn a good tool into one people keep installed.

| Rank | Feature | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unlimited-app pause with "Close app" as the primary action** | Strongest evidence (PNAS, DCCA). Paywalled past one app at one sec and Clearspace. The dismiss path is the active ingredient. |
| 2 | **Session budget at open, with a time's-up nudge** | Planning cut time 30.8% in the Danish RCT. It also counters friction's tendency to lengthen the sessions that remain. |
| 3 | **Surface-level Reels/Shorts/For You blocking that keeps DMs** | The feature users praise most and the clearest Android-only edge. Mostly sits in paid or ad-supported apps. Weak direct evidence, but very strong demand. |
| 4 | **Shared short-video allowance across apps (e.g., 10 min/day)** | Fits evidence that only low limits work and that users substitute between apps. Less brittle socially than a total block. |
| 5 | **Escalating delay for repeat opens** | Extends friction cheaply. A weak, escalating lockout was users' preferred strength in GoalKeeper. Free at ScreenZen only. |
| 6 | **Costly-not-impossible overrides (wait or typed reason), logged** | Fixes Digital Wellbeing's one-tap "ignore" and Samsung's one-tap "add time", while avoiding the emergency anxiety that strict lockouts cause. |
| 7 | **Snooze with automatic resume instead of disable** | Rebound after breaks is the best-documented failure mode. ADHD users ask for breaks that end on their own. |
| 8 | **Self-set, group-based daily limits (optional, low by suggestion)** | Allcott's −16%, sustained while active. Group scope blocks substitution. |
| 9 | **Smart 2-minute onboarding with presets and a mechanism explainer** | Setup is the first drop-off cliff. Explaining the mechanism nearly doubled retention in HabitLab. |
| 10 | **Reliability self-check plus OEM keep-alive guide** | The leading complaint is silent failure. A visible "protection OFF, tap to fix" banner turns silent churn into a fixable problem. |
| 11 | **Scheduled notification digests (3×/day) with hard guardrails** | Medium effects on attention and unlocks in an RCT. Rare outside Mindful and paid launchers. |
| 12 | **Routine schedules (school/work hours, evenings)** | Users who stick with blockers anchor them to routines. Automatic protection beats remembering to switch it on. |
| 13 | **Positive insights: dismissals, minutes reclaimed, weekly review** | Keeps motivation without streak resets or shame. Gives a natural moment to suggest stronger settings. |
| 14 | **Per-app grayscale (Shizuku) plus one-tap system grayscale schedules** | Moderate evidence of about 20 min/day saved, and it complements friction (it shortens sessions rather than preventing opens). Free in Curbox only. |
| 15 | **Optional minimalist launcher integrated with the core** | Gives aesthetics-driven users a calm home screen with screen time on it, while staying opt-in, revertible and widget-friendly to avoid known abandonment triggers. |

Browser URL blocking, Messages-only mode for Instagram, strict mode and two-week challenges just miss the list. They matter to specific segments (students, heavy users) but add policy or maintenance risk, or have thinner demand among casual users.

## Onboarding in under three minutes, with gentle defaults

The flow has to beat the two drop-off cliffs, permission setup and the first week, while meeting Play's prominent-disclosure rules. The guiding principle is to **ask for one permission at a time, and only when the user has chosen the feature that needs it**.

| Step | Screen | Purpose and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Use your phone on purpose. Free forever, no account, nothing leaves your phone." | States the trust promise up front. Accessibility privacy worries are a stated reason users want open source ([one sec reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US)). |
| 2 | "How it works": a 15-second animation showing the pause, the "Close" choice, and that "you can always open the app." | Mental-model explanation raised retention from 43% to 78% ([Kovacs 2018](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2018/habitlab/habitlab-cscw2018.pdf)). |
| 3 | Grant Usage Access (with a plain-language explainer), then "Your top attention apps last week," pre-checked from usage data. A manual picker is the fallback. | Zero-config protection. Usage Access is low-risk, but it is a known drop-off point ([DEV/Rexa](https://dev.to/rexa/how-to-block-apps-on-android-without-an-accessibilityservice-5gog)). |
| 4 | Choose an intensity: **Gentle / Balanced (recommended) / Firm** | User control and autonomy; middle strength is preferred ([GoalKeeper](https://ic.kaist.ac.kr/files/papers/kim2019goalkeeper.pdf)). |
| 5 | Grant "Display over other apps" and show a live demo pause on a chosen app | Shows the value immediately. |
| 6 | Optional: "Hide Reels and Shorts but keep your DMs?" leads to a full-screen prominent disclosure (what is read, that nothing is stored or sent) with "Agree" and "No thanks", then the Accessibility settings | Meets Play's separate, affirmative disclosure ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en)). Declining leaves the full UsageStats feature set. |
| 7 | Shown only on Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus or Huawei: a "Keep protection running" checklist with deep links | These OEMs rate 5/5 for background killing on [dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/). |
| 8 | Done. "We'll check in after a week." | Defer limits, digests, grayscale and the launcher to the day-7 review, so the user is not overloaded. |

Recommended defaults for the **Balanced** preset:

| Setting | Default | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Pause length | 8 s breathing animation; Gentle 5 s, Firm 15 s | Studies used 6 s; Pause Point uses about 10 s |
| Primary button | "Close [App]" (filled); "Continue" is secondary and appears after the delay | The dismiss option had the strongest effect |
| After "Continue" | Pick 5 / 10 / 15 minutes; nudge at expiry, then the pause comes back | DCCA Planning arm |
| Escalation | +5 s per extra open within 60 min, capped at 30 s | ScreenZen pattern; escalating friction preferred |
| Short-video surfaces (if enabled) | 10 min/day shared allowance, then that surface is blocked; DMs untouched | Low limits; substitution |
| Daily app limits | Off at install; offered in the day-7 review with a low suggested value | Budgeting backfire risk |
| Notification digests | Off; offered at day 7, at 3×/day | Avoids early overload |
| Grayscale | Off; offered as an "Advanced" Shizuku setup or a system-schedule deep link | High setup cost |
| Overrides | "Open anyway" after a 30 s wait (Firm: typed reason); always logged | Costly, not impossible |
| Snooze | 15 min / 1 h / until tomorrow; always auto-resumes | Rebound after breaks |
| Essentials | Phone, SMS, maps, banking, authenticators and camera are excluded from all rules | Users' top fear |
| Reflection prompt | Off (opt-in, every 5th open at most) | Weak evidence; annoyance |
| Streaks | None; weekly "calm days" summary | Streak evidence is negative or uncertain |

## Kotlin-native, tiered detection architecture

**Kotlin + Jetpack Compose, native only.** The core APIs (UsageStatsManager, AccessibilityService, NotificationListenerService, RoleManager) are Android-native. Mindful's Flutter stack must bridge them over platform channels, which is an inference about added complexity and not a measured cost. Kvaesitso is the best reference for a modern, modular Compose launcher codebase ([GitHub](https://github.com/MM2-0/Kvaesitso)), and Shorts-Blocker shows a clean pattern with one detector per app ([GitHub](https://github.com/atick-faisal/Shorts-Blocker)). A minSdk of 26 (Android 8) matches Curbox and Focus Launcher and covers the devices Pause Point leaves out.

| Module | Responsibility | Key technical choices |
|---|---|---|
| `core-usage` | Foreground-app detection without Accessibility | Poll `UsageStatsManager.queryEvents` about every 900 ms, **only while the screen is on**. Key on `ACTIVITY_RESUMED`/`MOVE_TO_FOREGROUND`. Advance the query window only after a successful read, cache the last package, and backdate the first window by about 2 s ([DEV/Rexa](https://dev.to/rexa/how-to-block-apps-on-android-without-an-accessibilityservice-5gog)). |
| `core-intervention` | Rules engine: pause, budget, escalation, limits, snooze, essentials | Pure Kotlin, unit-tested. Monotonic clocks (`elapsedRealtime`) plus a `TIME_CHANGED` receiver to defeat clock bypass. |
| `service` | Keeps detection alive | One low-importance `specialUse` FGS with a concrete subtype string ([developer.android.com](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/fgs-types-required)). Re-arm on `BOOT_COMPLETED` (verify on Android 15+). WorkManager for daily rollups. |
| `overlay` | Pause and block UI | Translucent, single-instance, excluded-from-recents activity started with SAW. De-duplicate launches (skip if already showing; same package within 1 s) ([DEV/Rexa](https://dev.to/rexa/how-to-block-apps-on-android-without-an-accessibilityservice-5gog)). Clear overlays during calls. |
| `a11y` (optional) | Instant detection, in-app surfaces, browser URLs | `android:packageNames` limited to the target apps; `TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED` and `TYPE_WINDOW_CONTENT_CHANGED`; debounced shallow tree walks; exit with a back action or overlay. Never `isAccessibilityTool`. |
| `detectors/*` (Apache-2.0) | Per-app surface fingerprints (view IDs, content descriptions, class names) | Several signals per surface to reduce breakage. **Recorded accessibility-tree fixtures** so contributors can fix a broken detector without the target app. Detectors fail open. |
| `notifications` (v1) | Digests | `NotificationListenerService`: snooze or cancel and store, then repost a digest at scheduled times. Skip ongoing notifications, calls, alarms and OTPs. |
| `display` (v1) | Grayscale | Optional Shizuku or ADB `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` toggling `accessibility_display_daltonizer` ([gist](https://gist.github.com/mrk-han/67a98616e43f86f8482c5ee6dd3faabe)). Fallback: system-mode deep links. |
| `launcher` (v1) | Optional HOME | `RoleManager.ROLE_HOME`. Holding the HOME role also grants sensitive-notification access on Android 15 ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-15-two-factor-authentication-codes-3492585)). Launcher interception only *complements* detection, because recents, notifications and deep links bypass it. |
| `data` | Local storage | Room for events and stats, DataStore for settings, encrypted export through the Storage Access Framework, migration tests. |
| Build flavors | `play` (no donate link), `foss` (F-Droid/Izzy/GitHub, donate link allowed) | No Firebase or GMS; ACRA or a local crash export instead ([F-Droid Inclusion Policy](https://f-droid.org/docs/Inclusion_Policy/)). Enumerate apps with a `<queries>` LAUNCHER intent, **not** `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` ([Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10158779?hl=en)). |

The hardest design trade-off is **updating detector rules versus having no INTERNET permission**. Pushing rules over the network fixes breakage within hours but adds an INTERNET permission and a server, which weakens the privacy pitch. Shipping rules only in app updates keeps the app offline but delays fixes. The recommendation is to stay offline in the core and keep a fast-track release lane for detector fixes, with rules stored as a versioned data file. A user can also import a signed rules file manually. A network rule-updater can come later as an explicit opt-in. If Accessibility usage changes in any material way, the Play declaration must be resubmitted ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en)).

## Earning trust: permission tiers that survive Play review and Android 17

Permissions are layered so that losing any one tier degrades the app gracefully rather than breaking it.

| Tier | Permission | Unlocks | Failure mode and mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | None | Launcher, focus timer UI, settings | None |
| 1 (core) | Usage Access + Display over other apps + `specialUse` FGS | Pause, budget, limits, stats, focus sessions | About 0.8–1 s detection latency. The Soccialstopper developer reported "no user-visible loss" ([DEV](https://dev.to/lui61140/why-i-removed-accessibilityservice-from-my-android-app-and-finally-got-into-play-store-4i52)). OEM killing is handled by the keep-alive guide and health check. |
| 2 (opt-in) | AccessibilityService | Reels/Shorts surfaces, feed filters, browser URLs, instant detection | Revoked by Android 17 Advanced Protection Mode ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-17-beta-2-advanced-protection-mode-accessibility-apps-3648860)). The app detects the revocation, falls back to Tier 1, and explains why. |
| 3 (opt-in) | Notification access | Digests, silencing | Battery savers can kill the listener; use `requestRebind()` and the health check. |
| 4 (advanced) | Shizuku/ADB `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` | Per-app grayscale | Setup friction; reportedly narrowing over Android versions (unverified). Fallback is a system-mode deep link. |

**AccessibilityService disclosure.** The app cannot claim `isAccessibilityTool`, since launchers and monitoring apps are explicitly excluded. It needs an in-app prominent disclosure shown during normal use and kept separate from other consent, affirmative consent, a Play Console declaration, and a video showing the consent flow, the decline flow and the core feature ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en)). The one documented rejection story is a warning: four rejections for "Missing prominent disclosure", and the developer said adding a disclosure screen did not help ([DEV](https://dev.to/lui61140/why-i-removed-accessibilityservice-from-my-android-app-and-finally-got-into-play-store-4i52)). Mindful's 50K–100K+ Play installs show an open-source wellbeing app can pass. The safest plan is to **submit the MVP in two steps**: first a Tier 1-only build to establish the listing, then the Accessibility feature with a polished declaration video. That way a rejection blocks one feature, not the app. Android 16's `accessibilityDataSensitive` flag can hide views from non-tool services ([Android Developers Blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/enhancing-android-security-stop-malware-from-snooping-on-your-app-data?hl=en)). Social apps are unlikely to set it on feeds, but that is an inference worth monitoring.

**Restricted settings and sideloading.** On Android 13, and more broadly on Android 15, apps installed from local files are blocked from Accessibility, notification access, usage access, overlay and device-admin grants until the user taps "Allow restricted settings" in App info ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-15-restricted-settings-sideloading-3481098)). Play installs and F-Droid/Izzy session-installer installs largely avoid this. GitHub APK users need an illustrated guide.

**Advanced Protection Mode.** AppBlock's only workaround is telling users to turn off AAPM ([AppBlock blog](https://appblock.app/androids-new-advanced-protection-affects-appblock-from-blocking/)). This app should never ask users to lower their security. It should say that Tier 1 features still work. That is where the UsageStats core pays off.

**OEM background killing.** Use a `specialUse` FGS, poll only while the screen is on, and keep a single quiet notification. Send users to `ACTION_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATION_SETTINGS` rather than the direct exemption prompt, which Play limits to acceptable use cases ([developer.android.com](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)). Show OEM-specific steps (Samsung's "Never sleeping apps", Xiaomi Autostart) ([dontkillmyapp Samsung](https://dontkillmyapp.com/samsung)), and re-check after firmware updates, since Samsung may re-add apps to its sleep lists.

**Tamper protection.** Avoid the common pattern of using Accessibility to bounce users out of the app's own uninstall or settings screens. It plausibly conflicts with Play's rules on user control (an inference; the specific policy text was not retrieved) and breaks across OEM Settings layouts. Device Admin adds only mild friction and is itself a restricted setting. Commitment should come from in-app mechanisms (delayed weakening, time-boxed strict sessions), not from preventing uninstall.

**Data safety and messaging.** Declare "no data collected or shared". With no INTERNET permission, that claim is verifiable, which turns the privacy unease users express about Accessibility into a reason to trust this app over its competitors.

## GPL-3.0, Play first, and no in-app donate button

**License: GPL-3.0-or-later for the app, Apache-2.0 for the detector library.** GPL-3.0 allows reuse of code from the strongest peers (Curbox, Olauncher, Kvaesitso and KISS are all GPL-3.0). It also stops closed forks from re-paywalling the work, which is a real risk in a market where clones charge $20–$100 a year. Apache-2.0 code can flow into GPLv3 but not the reverse ([ASF](https://www.apache.org/licenses/GPL-compatibility.html)). That makes an Apache-licensed detector library, following Kvaesitso's split between a GPL app and an Apache SDK, both reusable and grant-friendly. **Do not copy Mindful's GPL-2.0 code** unless it turns out to be "or later": FSF has never considered Apache-2.0 compatible with GPLv2. Use a DCO rather than a CLA.

## Conclusion

The winning product does not try to be a stronger wall. It is a **more reliable, more generous speed bump**: one that people forget is installed until the moment it saves them ten minutes, and that never costs them a DM, a bank login or an emergency call. Pause Point changes the competitive question. A plain pause is becoming an OS feature, so the open-source app should compete on feed-level precision, session budgeting, escalation and reach to every Android 8+ phone, and above all on not silently breaking. That shifts most of the engineering risk from the interventions, which are cheap, to the plumbing: detector maintenance, OEM survival and Play compliance. Those areas deserve the project's earliest and best investment.

The evidence also shows how to measure success. Effects last only while the tool runs, users loosen their settings over time, and the most effective designs (rotating, strict) lose users fastest. The real metric is therefore **months of active protection per user**, not minutes cut in week one. Most studies are short, small and linked to one sec. None tests Reels-only blocking or per-app grayscale. An open, privacy-preserving app is unusually well placed to fill that gap, for example by offering opt-in, on-device A/B presets whose results users can choose to share with independent researchers.
